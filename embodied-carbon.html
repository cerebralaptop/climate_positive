Embodied & Upfront Carbon

Reducing Carbon in
Materials & Construction

Embodied carbon from building materials and construction represents a growing share of emissions that must be urgently addressed. This page draws on GBCA’s research and practical guidance.

The Scale of the Problem

Embodied Carbon in Australia’s Buildings

Without action, embodied carbon will become the dominant source of building emissions as the electricity grid decarbonises.

22 Mt CO2e from embodied carbon in 2019
16% Share of building emissions in 2019
85% Projected share by 2050 under BAU
~25% Of a typical building’s lifetime emissions

The Growing Significance

In 2019, embodied carbon from Australia’s buildings totalled approximately 22 Mt CO2e, representing 16% of whole-life building carbon emissions. Under a business-as-usual scenario, this share is projected to climb to 85% by 2050.

This dramatic shift occurs because operational emissions will decline as the electricity grid decarbonises, but embodied carbon relies on process heat and chemical reactions in manufacturing that won’t be addressed by grid decarbonisation alone.

Immediate Impact

Unlike operational emissions that accrue over decades, embodied carbon is “spent” on day one when a building is constructed. These emissions can never be recovered. Even a 10% reduction in embodied emissions in new buildings would avoid at least 19.9 Mt CO2e by 2030 and 63.5 Mt CO2e by 2050.

Material efficiency improvements deliver emission savings immediately, unlike operational changes that depend on grid decarbonisation over time.

Definitions

Understanding Upfront Carbon

What is Upfront Carbon?

Upfront carbon emissions are produced during a building’s material production and construction activities before its use (life cycle stages A1–A5). These are tangible, measurable emissions reflecting past actions rather than future projections.

Upfront vs. Embodied Carbon

Embodied carbon includes upfront carbon (A1–A5) plus emissions from use, maintenance, refurbishment, and end-of-life stages (B1–B5, C1–C4). Upfront carbon is the portion with the highest certainty and greatest opportunity for reduction during design.

How to Calculate It

Calculating upfront carbon involves determining the quantity of each material and multiplying by its carbon intensity. Environmental Product Declarations (EPDs) provide product-specific data. GBCA’s Upfront Carbon Emissions Calculation Guide and the upcoming NABERS tool help standardise methodology.

Where is the Carbon?

In a typical building, superstructure accounts for ~42% of upfront carbon, substructure ~15%, and the building envelope ~13%. Key materials include concrete, steel, aluminium, glass, and insulation. Design decisions made early in the process have the greatest impact on reduction.

Green Star Targets

Upfront Carbon Reduction Targets

Green Star sets progressively increasing targets for upfront carbon reduction in new buildings.

10% Minimum reduction (2020 onwards)
20% Credit achievement target (from 2026)
40% Exceptional performance (by 2030)
Zero Net zero embodied carbon by 2050

Getting to Zero

Reducing upfront carbon is easiest during planning and early-stage design, where the largest reductions can be achieved with the least effort. As construction begins, options become more limited. The guide recommends reducing as much as possible through design and material selection, then offsetting any remaining emissions through nature-based solutions.

Policy Drivers

The IEA recommends building codes target net-zero material emissions. The NSW Government’s Sustainable Buildings SEPP introduces upfront carbon disclosure requirements. The City of Melbourne’s Zero Carbon Buildings Plan targets embodied energy reductions. NABERS is developing a benchmark tool for upfront carbon in buildings.

Reduction Strategies

How to Reduce Embodied Carbon

Both supply-side and demand-side strategies are needed. When combined, their benefits multiply.

Supply-Side Strategies

Improve Manufacturing Efficiency

Reduce energy intensity of manufacturing processes. Adopt best-practice production methods and invest in process optimisation.

Decarbonise Energy Supply

Switch manufacturing from fossil fuels to renewable electricity where possible. Address process heat through electrification or green hydrogen.

Reduce Process Emissions

Adopt lower-carbon cement chemistries, increase supplementary cementitious materials, use electric arc furnaces for steel, and explore carbon capture for hard-to-abate processes.

Demand-Side Strategies

Build with Less

Optimise structural design to reduce material quantities. Use lean design approaches, avoid over-engineering, and consider material-efficient structural systems.

Select Low-Carbon Materials

Specify materials with lower carbon intensity. Use EPDs to compare products. Consider mass timber, lower-carbon concrete, recycled steel, and bio-based materials.

Reuse and Recycle

Design for deconstruction and reuse. Maximise recycled content in materials. Minimise construction waste through prefabrication and careful planning.

Project Process

Reducing Carbon at Each Stage

The greatest reductions are possible in early design stages. As the project progresses, opportunities narrow.

Feasibility

Feasibility & Brief

  • Set upfront carbon reduction targets in the project brief
  • Engage sustainability consultants early to establish carbon budget
  • Consider adaptive reuse of existing structures vs new build
  • Include upfront carbon in tender and procurement requirements
Concept

Concept & Detailed Design

  • Optimise building form and structural system for material efficiency
  • Consider mass timber, post-tensioned concrete, or hybrid structures
  • Conduct preliminary LCA to identify carbon hotspots
  • Explore low-carbon alternatives for key materials
Tender

Tender & Procurement

  • Include embodied carbon requirements in tender documents
  • Request EPDs from suppliers for key materials
  • Evaluate tenders based on carbon performance alongside cost
  • Engage with MECLA for market-leading low-carbon supply
Build

Construction & Handover

  • Track actual vs predicted carbon through material quantities
  • Minimise waste and maximise recycled content on site
  • Document materials and products for as-built LCA
  • Offset remaining upfront carbon through nature-based solutions

Leading Practice

Case Studies

Commercial • Sydney, NSW

25 King Street

A landmark commercial tower that achieved significant upfront carbon reductions through optimised structural design, low-carbon concrete mixes, and recycled steel. Demonstrates that premium-grade offices can deliver ambitious embodied carbon targets.

Heritage Conversion • Melbourne, VIC

Substation No. 164

An adaptive reuse project converting a heritage electrical substation to commercial use. By retaining the existing structure, it avoided the significant embodied carbon of demolition and new construction while creating a distinctive workspace.

Commercial • Sydney, NSW

Quay Quarter Tower

A creative approach to embodied carbon: rather than demolishing the existing tower, 65% of the original structure was retained and integrated into the new design. This “upcycling” approach saved over 12,000 tonnes of embodied carbon compared to a new build.

Retail • Melbourne, VIC

Burwood Brickworks

Targeting the Living Building Challenge, this retail centre prioritised low-carbon materials and construction practices. Mass timber structural elements, recycled aggregate concrete, and responsible sourcing of all materials contributed to significant upfront carbon reductions.

Market & Policy

Driving Change

Green Star Buildings

Green Star Buildings (v1) requires at least 10% upfront carbon reduction for any rating. Higher ratings require 20% or more. Requirements will increase to 40% by 2030 to align with WorldGBC targets.

NABERS Embodied Emissions

NABERS, in partnership with GBCA, is developing a rating tool to benchmark and measure upfront carbon in buildings. This will provide an industry-standard method for comparison and driving reductions.

Climate Active

In 2022, Climate Active expanded its Carbon Neutral Standard to certify the upfront carbon of new buildings and major refurbishments. Groups like GPT have certified developments alongside their Green Star ratings.

MECLA

The Materials & Embodied Carbon Leaders’ Alliance brings together Australia’s leading construction companies and material suppliers to drive demand for low-carbon products and establish industry benchmarks.

TCFD Reporting

The Taskforce for Climate-related Financial Disclosure increasingly recognises Scope 3 emissions, which include upfront carbon, as a material risk. This is driving disclosure and action by property companies.

CEFC Investment

The Clean Energy Finance Corporation is investing in embodied carbon reduction, including programs to promote mass engineered timber as a lower-carbon alternative to conventional construction.

Every New Building is an Opportunity

Upfront carbon can never be recovered once a building is built. The time to act is during design and construction — the decisions made today lock in emissions forever.
