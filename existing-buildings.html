Existing Buildings

Climate Positive Pathway
for Existing Buildings

Existing buildings represent the largest share of emissions in the built environment. Over the next 30 years, millions of buildings must be transformed to achieve climate positive outcomes.

Our Vision

Decarbonising Existing Buildings

All existing buildings in Australia must be Climate Positive by 2050. All Green Star Performance rated buildings must achieve this by 2040. World-leading buildings must achieve this by 2030.

2030 World-leading buildings climate positive
2040 All Green Star Performance buildings
2050 All existing buildings in Australia

Why Focus on Existing Buildings?

Existing buildings are the largest share of emissions in the built environment. Most emissions come from energy use (electricity, gas, and diesel), with additional emissions from embodied carbon in maintenance and refurbishment, water, and waste.

Critically, most buildings standing today will still be operational in 2050. They can decarbonise, but this takes time and deliberate action. Over the next 30 years, millions of buildings will need to become more energy efficient, replace fossil fuel equipment, and ensure high comfort levels for occupants.

The Challenge

Unlike new buildings that can be designed climate positive from the start, existing buildings face unique constraints:

  • Ageing infrastructure and systems designed around fossil fuels
  • Tenant and lease constraints limiting retrofit scope
  • Capital expenditure needed for equipment replacement
  • Complexity of managing occupied buildings during upgrades
  • Split incentives between building owners and tenants

The Tool

Green Star Performance

Green Star Performance drives the acceleration of decarbonisation in existing buildings through clear targets and a structured pathway.

What is Green Star Performance?

Green Star Performance is GBCA’s rating tool for existing buildings in operation. It provides an annual assessment framework covering energy, water, waste, indoor environment quality, and management. The tool sets progressively ambitious targets for decarbonisation, driving transformation across the building stock.

Unlike design-stage tools, Performance ratings assess real operational data, ensuring buildings deliver on their sustainability promises in practice.

The Climate Positive Pathway

Green Star Performance v2 introduces a Climate Positive Pathway with targets for six key emission categories. Buildings must progressively address each category to achieve higher ratings and ultimately reach climate positive status.

The pathway provides clear definitions and deadlines, giving industry certainty about what is expected and when. This enables long-term planning and investment in building upgrades.

Six Categories

Emission Categories & Targets

The pathway addresses six distinct emission categories, each with specific targets and timelines.

Energy Source

Transition to 100% renewable electricity. Options include on-site solar, power purchase agreements (PPAs), GreenPower, and large-scale generation certificates (LGCs). By 2030, all world-leading buildings must source 100% renewable electricity.

Target: 100% renewable electricity

Energy Use

Reduce operational energy consumption through efficiency upgrades, retro-commissioning, and smart building management. Benchmark against NABERS Energy ratings. Continuous improvement in energy performance is expected at each rating cycle.

Target: Continuous efficiency improvement

Embodied Carbon

Address the emissions from materials and products used in maintenance, refurbishment, and fit-out activities. Specify low-carbon materials for all replacement and upgrade works. Track and report embodied carbon in major refurbishments.

Target: Measure and reduce

Other Carbon Emissions

Address Scope 1 emissions from base building operations, including refrigerant leakage, diesel backup generators, and any remaining gas use. Phase out high-GWP refrigerants. Eliminate fossil fuel use in base building services.

Target: Eliminate or offset all Scope 1

Tenant Energy Source

Engage tenants in the transition to renewable energy. Provide green energy options in leases. Support tenants to procure renewable electricity. By 2035, all tenant electricity in Green Star buildings should be from renewable sources.

Target: 100% renewable tenant electricity

Other Tenant Emissions

Address tenant Scope 1 and 3 emissions including gas use in tenant spaces, tenant waste, and water. Support tenants to electrify their spaces. Provide facilities for waste reduction and water efficiency.

Target: Reduce and offset residual

Milestones

The Pathway Timeline

Progressive targets from now through to 2040, when all Green Star Performance buildings must be climate positive.

2023

Foundation Actions

  • All Green Star Performance buildings install smart metering and monitoring
  • 6 Star buildings source 100% renewable electricity (base building)
  • Develop fossil fuel elimination transition plans for all rated buildings
  • Begin measuring and reporting embodied carbon in major refurbishments
2026

Accelerating Change

  • 5 Star and above source 100% renewable electricity
  • All rated buildings have smart energy monitoring systems
  • Fossil fuels eliminated from cooking in all rated fitouts
  • Low-GWP refrigerant transition plans in place for all buildings
2030

World-Leading Buildings Climate Positive

  • All rated buildings source 100% renewable electricity (base building)
  • 6 Star buildings are fully fossil fuel-free
  • All Scope 1 emissions in world-leading buildings eliminated or offset
  • Tenant energy transition programs established in all rated buildings
2035

Nearing Climate Positive

  • All rated buildings fully fossil fuel-free (base building and tenants)
  • Tenant electricity 100% renewable across all rated buildings
  • Embodied carbon from refurbishment activities being actively reduced
  • Remaining emissions being offset through nature-based solutions
2040

All Green Star Buildings Climate Positive

  • All Green Star Performance rated buildings are climate positive
  • All emissions either eliminated or compensated through high-quality offsets
  • Buildings fully electrified, efficient, and powered by 100% renewables
  • Setting the example for the remaining building stock to follow by 2050

Taking Action

Key Action Areas for Building Owners

Electrify Building Services

Replace gas boilers, gas hot water, and gas cooking with efficient electric alternatives. Align replacements with equipment end-of-life to manage costs. Plan electrical infrastructure upgrades to accommodate new electric loads. See our Electrification guide for detailed guidance.

Procure Renewable Energy

Source 100% renewable electricity through a combination of on-site solar, corporate PPAs, GreenPower, and LGCs. Maximise on-site generation to reduce grid dependence and energy costs. Consider community energy and renewable energy aggregation.

Upgrade Building Efficiency

Invest in building performance upgrades: LED lighting, efficient HVAC, improved insulation, smart building management systems, and retro-commissioning. Target progressive improvement in NABERS Energy ratings over time.

Engage Tenants

Collaborate with tenants on green lease clauses, shared sustainability targets, and energy data transparency. Support tenants to electrify their spaces and procure renewable energy. Address the split incentive challenge through green lease frameworks.

Phase Out Refrigerants

Develop a plan to transition from high-GWP HFC refrigerants to low-GWP alternatives (natural refrigerants, HFOs). Align transitions with HVAC system replacements. Minimise refrigerant leakage through improved maintenance.

Offset Residual Emissions

For emissions that cannot yet be eliminated, procure high-quality nature-based carbon offsets. Follow the “Last but not later” framework to ensure offset integrity. Progressively reduce reliance on offsets as abatement options expand.

Looking Forward

The Next Steps

Green Star Future Focus

GBCA’s Future Focus program will extend climate positive requirements beyond commercial offices to homes, fitouts, precincts, and communities by 2030. This will ensure the entire built environment, not just the leading edge, is on a pathway to climate positive.

Policy and Regulation

Government policy must support the decarbonisation of existing buildings through building performance standards, energy efficiency requirements, fossil fuel phase-out timelines, and financial incentives for building upgrades. Mandatory disclosure of building energy and emissions performance will drive market transformation.
