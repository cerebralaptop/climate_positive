Offset Framework

Carbon Offsets:
Last But Not Later

A framework for ensuring the environmental integrity of carbon offsets in the property sector. Offsets should be applied last in the mitigation sequence but not delayed due to market complexity.

The Principle

The Role of Offsets in Net Zero

Offsets are a compensatory measure for residual emissions that cannot yet be eliminated. They must complement — never replace — direct emission reductions.

“Last But Not Later”

The framework takes a “last, but not later” approach: offsets are applied last in the sequence of carbon mitigation — after reducing energy use, switching to renewables, and eliminating fossil fuels — but should not be delayed because of the complexities in determining offset integrity.

During the transition period, some emissions are harder to abate than others. Where residual emissions exist, high-quality offsets can deliver environmental benefits as a compensatory measure while organisations work to eliminate those emissions entirely.

Classification

The Oxford Taxonomy of Offsets

The framework is guided by the Oxford Principles for Net Zero Aligned Carbon Offsetting, which classify offsets by their mechanism and permanence.

Emission Reduction Offsets

These offsets prevent emissions that would otherwise have occurred. Examples include renewable energy projects that displace fossil fuel generation, and avoided deforestation (REDD+). While valuable, these do not remove carbon from the atmosphere.

Over time, the proportion of these offsets should decrease as the global economy decarbonises and there are fewer emissions to “avoid.”

Carbon Removal Offsets

These offsets actively remove carbon dioxide from the atmosphere. Nature-based removals include afforestation, reforestation, soil carbon sequestration, and wetland restoration. Engineered removals include direct air capture and enhanced weathering.

The framework recommends increasing the share of carbon removal offsets over time, ultimately reaching 100% removals by mid-century.

The Shift to Removals

Aligned with the Oxford Principles, users should progressively shift from emission reduction offsets to carbon removal offsets. Short-lived storage (e.g., forestry) should transition to long-lived storage (e.g., geological) over time. By 2050, all offsets should be carbon removals with long-duration storage.

Step by Step

Developing an Offset Strategy

A structured approach to integrating high-quality offsets into your net zero pathway.

Set Boundaries & Baseline

Define which emissions are within scope (Scope 1, 2, and relevant Scope 3). Establish a robust emissions baseline. Identify which emissions can be directly abated and which require offsets.

Prioritise Direct Reductions

Implement all feasible direct emission reduction measures first. Electrification, energy efficiency, and renewable energy procurement should take priority. Offsets address only the residual emissions gap.

Select High-Quality Offsets

Apply due diligence criteria to evaluate offset quality. Prioritise nature-based removals with co-benefits. Ensure offsets are certified under recognised standards (VCS, Gold Standard, Australian ERF).

Monitor, Report & Evolve

Track offset quality and impact over time. Report transparently on offset use in sustainability disclosures. Progressively reduce reliance on offsets as direct abatement options expand.

Due Diligence

Offset Quality Criteria

The framework identifies key criteria that property organisations should evaluate when assessing offset quality.

1

Additionality

Would the emission reduction or removal have occurred without the offset funding? The project must be additional to what would have happened anyway.

2

Permanence

How long will the carbon be stored? Forest offsets face reversal risks from fire or disease. Long-term storage mechanisms provide greater certainty.

3

Measurability

Can the emission reductions be accurately measured and verified? Robust monitoring, reporting, and verification (MRV) is essential.

4

No Leakage

Does the project simply shift emissions elsewhere? Protecting one forest should not lead to increased deforestation in another location.

5

Third-Party Verification

Is the offset verified by an independent third party under a recognised standard? Certification provides assurance of quality and accountability.

6

Transparency

Is information about the project publicly available? Transparent projects allow stakeholders to assess the quality and impact of the offset.

7

Co-Benefits

Does the project deliver additional social and environmental benefits? Biodiversity, community livelihoods, water management, and ecosystem services add value.

8

No Double Counting

Is the offset retirement tracked to prevent the same reduction being claimed by multiple parties? Registry systems are essential to avoid double counting.

Preferred Approach

Nature-Based Solutions

Green Star prioritises nature-based offsets that deliver carbon removal alongside broader environmental and social benefits.

Afforestation & Reforestation

Planting new forests or restoring degraded forests to sequester carbon. These projects can also restore biodiversity, improve water quality, and provide habitat corridors. Eligible under VCS, Gold Standard, and Australian ERF.

Agricultural Land Management

Improving soil carbon through regenerative agriculture practices. This includes cover cropping, reduced tillage, rotational grazing, and biochar application. Supports food production while drawing down carbon.

Wetlands & Blue Carbon

Restoring coastal wetlands, mangroves, and seagrass meadows. These ecosystems can store carbon at rates far exceeding terrestrial forests while also providing flood protection, fisheries habitat, and coastal resilience.

Improved Forest Management

Managing existing forests to increase carbon stocks through practices such as extended rotation lengths, reduced-impact logging, and protection of old-growth areas.

Savanna Fire Management

Early dry season burning in northern Australia reduces the severity of late-season wildfires, resulting in net emission reductions. These projects also support Indigenous land management and cultural practices.

Urban Green Infrastructure

On-site nature-based solutions such as urban forests, green roofs, and biodiverse landscapes can contribute to precinct-level carbon sequestration while improving amenity, cooling, and water management.

Certification

Offsets in Green Star

How Green Star Uses Offsets

Green Star allows high-quality nature-based offsets for selected emission scopes. The approach is aligned with the Oxford Principles and requires offsets to be from nature-based carbon removal projects.

Eligible offset standards include Verra’s Verified Carbon Standard (VCS), Gold Standard, the Climate, Community & Biodiversity Alliance (CCBA), and the Australian Emissions Reduction Fund (ERF).

Eligible Emission Scopes

Green Star allows offsets for:

  • Scope 1 emissions (e.g., refrigerant leakage) that cannot be eliminated
  • Upfront carbon emissions remaining after reduction efforts
  • Other hard-to-abate emissions at the asset level

Offsets are not accepted as a substitute for energy efficiency, renewable energy, or fossil fuel elimination.

High-Quality Offsets Are Part of the Solution

When applied with integrity after direct reduction efforts, nature-based offsets deliver real climate benefits alongside biodiversity, community, and resilience outcomes.
