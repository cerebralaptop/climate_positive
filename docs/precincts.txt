From flooding in Europe to fires
in our own backyard, it has

isn’t coming – it is upon us.

In fact, the report notes that
at the current pace, we will
exhaust our capacity to meet any
reasonable climate target.

notes that the changes that we
are seeing are happening with an
average increase of 1.1 degrees over
pre-industrial levels. We are also
on a trajectory to exceed 1.5ºC over
the next two decades. The effects
of this warming will be dangerous,
costly, and potentially irreversible.

we need to do to try and limit
climate change to 1.5ºC:

The timeframes for a precinct’s
planning, design, construction and
operation are diverse. Some precinct
developments may take less than
five years from planning to full build
out. Others can take many years to
plan and design, and then decades

for a staged approach to construction
and handover. Some campus style
precincts are continually evolving
in response to new challenges and
opportunities to meet the needs
of their owners and users.

Firstly, infrastructure can contribute
to a precinct’s carbon impact
through its design, construction and
operation. Secondly, it can underpin
the ability for buildings and other
assets to reduce their emissions by
providing more efficient, low carbon
energy and utility supply. And thirdly,

it can provide low carbon choices
for users of the precinct - such as
low carbon transport systems.

A net zero carbon precinct is one where there is a balance between the
amount of greenhouse gas produced and the amount removed from
the atmosphere on a net annual basis. It is a technical, though openended term. Its use must be accompanied by the focus on emissions,
e.g. net zero carbon in operations, net zero carbon for construction,
etc. Net zero carbon claims are often unverified, and at this stage,
there is no agreed standard for precincts that defines them.

Carbon neutral precinct
A carbon neutral precinct, as defined in the Climate Active
Carbon Neutral Standard for Precincts, is one where the
operations of the precinct result in net zero emissions, typically
through a mix of emissions reductions and offset projects to eliminate
remaining emissions. The standard draws on international protocols
for accounting of scope 1,2 and 3 emissions, and applies them across
a cycle of activities: measure, reduce, offset, validate and report.
Carbon neutral certification against the Precinct Standard
can be sought through the Australian Government.

Climate Positive precinct
Climate positive precincts use the principles to identify and deliver on
carbon reductions in the planning, design, construction and operation of
the precinct. These principles are complementary to the climate positive
principles applied at a building scale, as outlined in the Climate Positive
Roadmap for buildings, and integrated into Green Star Buildings.
As the Green Star Communities rating tool is updated, a Climate Positive
Pathway will be developed to align with the principles, providing projects
with a more prescriptive set of requirements (in line with the targets in this
document) to meet the pathway and the assurance of the independent
third party verification that Green Star certification provides.

Improving residential

Green Star Homes is GBCA’s rating
tool for single dwelling homes and
townhouses. It seeks to create highly
efficient, fossil fuel free homes,
powered by renewables that are
healthy and resilient for all Australians.
Green Star Homes has three
categories focusing on core outcomes
that define what a sustainable,
healthy, and resilient home is.
Healthy: Promotes actions and
solutions that improve the quality
of the home. Green Star Homes are
ventilated, comfortable, and made
with products that are better for you.
Resilient: Encourages solutions
to future proof the home. Green
Star Homes are water efficient
and climate change ready.
Positive: Sets out a path for homes
to be highly efficient and powered
by renewables. Green Star Homes
are fully electric, draught sealed,
efficient, and powered by renewables.
In its initial version, Green Star homes
targets volume home builders who
deliver large amount of housing, as
a way of maximising uptake and
driving better outcomes at scale. This
gives many residential developers
seeking climate positive outcomes the
opportunity to align their approach
to carbon and sustainability at a
building and precinct scale. And
with an all electric approach to
homes, contributing to the vision
of fossil fuel free precincts.

Green Star Homes will give
residential precinct developers the
opportunity to collaborate directly
and meaningfully with volume home
builders like never before: delivery
of low carbon, fossil fuel free and
sustainable homes that contribute to
the climate positive precinct approach.
When delivered in tandem with
Green Star Communities, it creates a
compelling and consistent message
for consumers about the aspirations
of the community they are buying
into - with the assurance that only the
Green Star trademark can provide.
This will be supported with efforts
led by GBCA to promote certified
homes in the marketplace and
raise awareness with consumers
of the benefits that Green Star
Certified homes will bring, furthering
demand and uptake. This work will
also support users of Green Star
Communities on how to promote the
benefits of their project to consumers.
Central to this will be the vision for
climate positive precincts, and the
opportunities for occupants of these
certified precincts to live low carbon
lifestyles as individuals, families,
and neighbourhood communities.
